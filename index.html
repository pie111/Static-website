I’m a software engineer at IBM, living in Kerala. Since July 2022 I’ve been working on WatsonX Orchestrate, which, put simply, is software that helps people build their own AI agents just by describing what they want.

Most days I’m somewhere between Python, Postgres and a whiteboard. On the side I build small tools for myself, like DB-Pal, which lets you talk to your database in plain English.

The story so far

In two short chapters.

Chapter one · 2018 – 2022

College, and a tiny company on the side

I studied Electronics and Communication at NSS College of Engineering in Akathethara. Somewhere in the middle of it, I co-founded TeamBery, a small remote studio where I worked part-time during college. We built websites and web apps in React and Node for a handful of small businesses, mostly to take boring manual work off their plates.

I did a bit of everything there: talking to clients, deciding what to build, designing it, writing it and putting it live. It taught me early that the hard part is rarely the code. It’s figuring out what people actually need.

Chapter two · 2022 – now

IBM, and teaching software to build software

I joined IBM India Software Labs in Kochi straight out of college and have been on WatsonX Orchestrate ever since. The thing I’m proudest of is an agent builder, built on LangGraph, that lets business users create their own AI agents by just talking to it. More than a hundred enterprise customers use it now.

Along the way I rebuilt a big piece of the platform from scratch so other teams could ship without waiting on each other, made search over company knowledge noticeably smarter with pgvector, chased down slow endpoints with Redis and better SQL, and helped move the whole thing from AWS to IBM Cloud without anyone noticing downtime. I’ve also had the fun of helping three newer engineers find their feet.

IBM was kind enough to give me its Outstanding Technical Achievement Award two years in a row, and a Growth Award too.
